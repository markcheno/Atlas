Airworthiness Directives; The Boeing Company Airplanes

AGENCY: Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), DOT.

ACTION: Final rule.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------

SUMMARY: The FAA is adopting a new airworthiness directive (AD) for 
certain The Boeing Company Model 787-8, 787-9, and 787-10 airplanes. 
This AD was prompted by reports that the low range radio altimeter 
(LRRA) system has an increased rate of non-computed data (NCD) outputs 
at low altitudes during approach and landing, which could cause 
unreliable altimeter function. This AD requires replacing the left and 
right LRRA transmit and receive coaxial cables with larger gauge 
coaxial cables. The FAA is issuing this AD to address the unsafe 
condition on these products.

DATES: This AD is effective December 14, 2026.

SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION:

Background

    The FAA issued a notice of proposed rulemaking (NPRM) to amend 14 
CFR part 39 by adding an AD that would apply to certain The Boeing 
Company Model 787-8, 787-9, and 787-10 airplanes. The NPRM was 
published in the Federal Register on February 24, 2026 (91 FR 8794). 
The NPRM was prompted by reports that the LRRA system has an increased 
rate of NCD outputs at low altitudes during approach and landing, which 
could cause unreliable altimeter function. In the NPRM, the FAA 
proposed to require replacing the left and right LRRA transmit and 
receive coaxial cables with larger gauge coaxial cables. The FAA is 
issuing this AD to address unreliable data output or in-service 
disruptions of LRRA functions. The unsafe condition, if not addressed, 
could result in loss of continued safe flight and landing.

Discussion of Final Airworthiness Directive

Comments

    The FAA received comments from the Air Line Pilots Association, 
International (ALPA) and Boeing who supported the NPRM without change.
    The FAA received additional comments from seven commenters, 
including Air Canada, American Airlines (American), EVA Air, the 
Foundation for Aviation Safety, Turkish Airlines, United Airlines 
(United), and an anonymous commenter. The following presents those 
comments and the FAA's response.

Request To Reference Revised Service Information

    Air Canada and United requested the FAA revise the proposed AD to 
specify that Boeing Alert Service Bulletin B787-81205-SB340062-00, 
Issue 002, dated August 11, 2025 (Issue 002 of the Service Bulletin) 
may be used to accomplish the proposed actions. United noted that Issue 
002 of the Service Bulletin corrects the required material kits and 
consumables and updates the installation instructions.
    EVA Air requested that the FAA confirm accomplishment of Boeing 
Alert Requirements Bulletin B787-81205-SB340062-00 RB, Issue 002, dated 
August 11, 2025 (Issue 002 of the Requirements Bulletin), is acceptable 
for compliance with the proposed AD. EVA stated it identified multiple 
deviations and practical implementation issues with Boeing Alert 
Requirements Bulletin B787-81205-SB340062-00 RB, Issue 001, dated 
December 23, 2024 (Issue 001 of the Requirements Bulletin).
    Turkish Airlines requested that the FAA revise the applicability of 
the proposed AD to reference Issue 002 of the Requirements Bulletin. 
Turkish Airlines stated that, due to technical problems in Issue 001 of 
the Requirements Bulletin, it accomplished some actions using Issue 001 
along with approved repair data, and continued implementation using 
Issue 002 of the Requirements Bulletin.
    The FAA agrees that Issue 002 of the Service Bulletin may be used 
for accomplishing the actions required by

[[Page 70775]]

this AD. Tables 1 and 2 of the Accomplishment Instructions of Issue 001 
of the Requirements Bulletin refer to Issue 001 of the Service Bulletin 
or ``later approved issues'' as accepted procedures. Further, the FAA 
reviewed Issue 002 of the Service Bulletin, which updates work 
instructions for Group 4 airplanes, revises task tables and 
illustrations, and updates industry support information without 
expanding the scope of the AD. Therefore, operators may use the 
procedures in Issue 002 of the Service Bulletin for compliance with 
requirements of this AD. The FAA has determined no change to this AD is 
necessary in regard to Air Canada's and United's requests.
    The FAA disagrees with EVA Air's and Turkish Airline's requests. 
Changes in Issue 002 of the Service Bulletin do not affect the required 
for compliance (RC) steps in Issue 001 of the Requirements Bulletin. 
Therefore, Boeing has not released Issue 002 of the Requirements 
Bulletin. However, as noted in the previous paragraph, later approved 
issues of the service bulletin provide accepted procedures, and this 
meets the intent of the requests from EVA Air and Turkish Airlines.

Request To Extend Compliance Time

    American, Turkish Airlines, and an anonymous commenter requested 
that the FAA extend the compliance time of the proposed AD. American 
stated that Boeing quoted a lead time exceeding 500 days for the 
required cables, which would shorten the available time to accomplish 
the proposed requirements. Turkish Airlines stated that it shared its 
kit needs with Boeing in March 2025 but continued to experience kit 
supply problems. The anonymous commenter stated that moving the 
compliance date slightly after the effective date of the final rule 
would allow operators adequate time to incorporate the required 
modifications into scheduled maintenance cycles without unnecessary 
operational disruption.
    The FAA disagrees with extending the compliance time. In developing 
an appropriate compliance time for this action, the FAA considered the 
recommendations of the manufacturer, the urgency associated with the 
subject unsafe condition, the availability of required parts, and the 
practical aspect of accomplishing the required modification within a 
period of time that corresponds to the normal scheduled maintenance for 
most affected operators. Further, the FAA has determined that operators 
have sufficient time within the 5-year compliance period to accomplish 
the required coaxial cable installation. However, under the provisions 
of paragraph (i) of this AD, the FAA will consider requests for 
approval of an extension of the compliance time if sufficient data are 
submitted to substantiate that the new compliance time would provide an 
acceptable level of safety. The FAA has not changed this AD in regard 
to this comment.

Request To Reduce Compliance Time

    The Foundation for Aviation Safety noted that the unsafe condition 
is critical and questioned whether it is necessary to allow up to five 
years to accomplish the proposed actions that are estimated to take 
approximately 28 hours.
    As discussed in the previous comment, the FAA has determined that 
the compliance time, as proposed, represents an appropriate amount of 
time in which the required actions can be performed in a timely manner 
within the affected fleet while still maintaining an adequate level of 
safety. The FAA has not changed this AD in regard to this comment.

Request To Allow Continued Operation Under Visual Flight Rules (VFR)

    An anonymous commenter requested that the FAA consider permitting 
continued operation of affected airplanes on routes where both the 
departure and arrival airports are forecasted to maintain VFR 
conditions, since pilots operating under VFR rely primarily on visual 
references rather than sole dependence on radio altimeter data during 
approach and landing. The commenter stated this approach would maintain 
safety objectives while minimizing economic and scheduling impacts on 
operators and passengers during the transition period.
    The FAA disagrees because the FAA has determined that LRRA coaxial 
cables must be replaced to address the unsafe condition. The LRRA is 
needed for everyday operations including continued operation of 
affected aircraft on routes where both departure and arrival airports 
are forecast to maintain VFR conditions. Further, this AD does not 
prohibit operations of the affected airplanes. The FAA has not changed 
this AD in regard to this comment.

Additional Changes Made to This Final Rule

    Issue 002 of the Service Bulletin includes an update to the 
estimated work-hours. Therefore, the FAA has revised the Costs of 
Compliance section in this final rule.

Conclusion

    The FAA reviewed the relevant data, considered any comments 
received, and determined that air safety requires adopting this AD as 
proposed. Accordingly, the FAA is issuing this AD to address the unsafe 
condition on these products. Except for minor editorial changes, and 
any other changes described previously, this AD is adopted as proposed 
in the NPRM. None of the changes will increase the economic burden on 
any operator.

Authority for This Rulemaking

    Title 49 of the United States Code specifies the FAA's authority to 
issue rules on aviation safety. Subtitle I, section 106, describes the 
authority of the FAA Administrator. Subtitle VII: Aviation Programs, 
describes in more detail the scope of the Agency's authority.
    The FAA is issuing this rulemaking under the authority described in 
Subtitle VII, Part A, Subpart III, Section 44701: General requirements. 
Under that section, Congress charges the FAA with promoting safe flight 
of civil aircraft in air commerce by prescribing regulations for 
practices, methods, and procedures the Administrator finds necessary 
for safety in air commerce. This regulation is within the scope of that 
authority because it addresses an unsafe condition that is likely to 
exist or develop on products identified in this rulemaking action.

    This AD applies to The Boeing Company Model 787-8, 787-9, and

(e) Unsafe Condition

    This AD was prompted by reports that the low range radio 
altimeter (LRRA) system has an increased rate of non-computed data 
(NCD) outputs at low altitudes during approach and landing. The FAA 
is issuing this AD to address unreliable data output or in-service

addressed, could result in loss of continued safe flight and 
landing.

(f) Compliance

    Comply with this AD within the compliance times specified, 
unless already done.

    Except as specified by paragraph (h) of this AD: At the 
applicable times specified in the ``Compliance'' paragraph of Boeing

and in accordance with, the Accomplishment Instructions of Boeing 
Alert Requirements Bulletin B787-81205-SB340062-00 RB, Issue 001, 
dated December 23, 2024.
    Note 1 to paragraph (g): Guidance for accomplishing the actions 
required by this AD can be found in Boeing Alert Service Bulletin 
B787-81205-SB340062-00, Issue 01, dated December 23, 2024, which is 
referred to in Boeing Alert Requirements Bulletin B787-81205-
SB340062-00 RB, Issue 001, dated December 23, 2024.

    Where the Compliance Time columns of the tables in the 
``Compliance'' paragraph of Boeing Alert Requirements Bulletin B787-
81205-SB340062-00 RB, Issue 001, dated December 23, 2024, refer to
